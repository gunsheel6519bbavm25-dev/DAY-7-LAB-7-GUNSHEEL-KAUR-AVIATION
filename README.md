Objective

The objective of this lab is to understand how Generative AI can be used as a guided drafting assistant for professional aviation emails.

The lab focuses on structured email prompting, BLUF and PAS communication frameworks, review and revision, audience-based tone adaptation, and the importance of human approval before using AI-generated business communication.

AI-Assisted Workplace Writing Process

The guided drafting process followed in this lab is:

Business Need → Structured Prompt → AI Draft → Human Review → Revision → Approval

Generative AI should support the drafting process rather than independently approving or sending important business communication.

Six-Part Email Prompt

A professional aviation email prompt contains six components:

Role – Defines the professional perspective.
Task – Explains what the AI needs to write.
Context – Provides relevant background information.
Constraints – Defines rules and limitations.
Output Format – Defines the required structure.
Tone – Defines how the message should sound.
Reusable Structure
Role: Airline customer-service communication executive
Task: Draft a professional flight-delay email
Context: Use verified information about Flight AV408
Constraints: Do not include unsupported compensation, refund, meal, hotel or gate information.
Output Format: Professional passenger email using BLUF
Tone: Calm, professional, reassuring and empathetic
BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

BLUF means Bottom Line Up Front.

It places the most important information at the beginning of the message.

BLUF is useful for:
Flight-delay communication
Gate changes
Flight cancellations
Operational updates
Urgent internal emails
BLUF Structure
Main information
Supporting details
Required action
Contact or verification instruction
PAS — Problem, Agitation and Solution

PAS stands for:

Problem
Agitation
Solution

PAS is generally used for persuasive or marketing communication.

In aviation communication, PAS should be used carefully because exaggerating passenger concerns may create unnecessary fear or anxiety.

Six-Part Structured Prompt

A structured prompt was created using all six components:

Act as an airline customer-service communication executive. Draft a professional email informing passengers of Flight AV408 from Delhi to Bengaluru about a two-hour delay caused by adverse weather. The original departure time was 3:00 PM, and the revised departure time is 5:00 PM. Ask passengers to monitor the airline's official application and airport display screens for further updates. Use the BLUF structure and keep the email below 130 words. Use a calm, professional and reassuring tone. Do not invent a gate number or mention compensation, refunds, meal arrangements, hotel accommodation or other policy benefits.

Structured Email Output

Subject: Flight AV408 – Departure Delayed

Flight AV408 from Delhi to Bengaluru is delayed by two hours due to adverse weather. The revised departure time is 5:00 PM instead of 3:00 PM.

Passengers should continue to monitor the airline's official application and airport display screens for the latest updates.

The message uses a calm, professional and reassuring tone while avoiding unsupported compensation, refund, meal, hotel or gate information.

Four-Dimension Review Gate

The generated email was reviewed using four dimensions:

1. Accuracy

Verify flight number, route, delay and revised departure time using official operational information.

2. Tone

The communication should remain reassuring without overpromising or using exaggerated language.

3. Compliance

Unsupported compensation, refund, meal, hotel and other policy statements must be avoided.

4. Context

Passengers need clear and concise next steps, such as checking the official airline application and airport display screens.

Targeted Revision

The email was improved through targeted follow-up prompts.

The revisions included:

Moving the revised departure time to the opening.
Removing unsupported compensation or passenger benefits.
Using a reassuring but non-committal tone.
Reducing the email length.
Using [VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SYSTEM] where operational information is missing.
Passenger Complaint Response
Scenario

A passenger reports that:

Their flight was delayed for three hours.
They missed an important meeting.
Airport staff did not provide clear information.
No compensation or refund policy was supplied.
Communication Approach

The response should:

Acknowledge the inconvenience.
Show empathy.
Avoid admitting legal liability.
Avoid promising compensation or refunds.
Avoid inventing airline policy.
Direct the passenger to an authorised support channel.
Maintain a calm, respectful and professional tone.
Internal Aviation Email
Scenario

Passenger queues at security screening are longer than usual, and additional staff have been assigned to assist with queue management.

Communication Requirements

The internal email should:

State the main issue first.
Use BLUF.
Avoid blaming any department.
Provide clear staff instructions.
Remain below the specified word limit.
Use a clear, professional and action-oriented tone.
Tone Adaptation

The intended audience affects the communication style.

Audience	Required Tone	Information Priority
Passengers	Calm, reassuring and empathetic	Delay, revised time and instructions
Senior Management	Concise and factual	Operational status and impact
Operations Staff	Direct and action-oriented	Instructions and coordination

The same operational information can therefore be communicated differently depending on the audience.

Peer Review

The final email was reviewed for:

Accuracy of facts
BLUF application
Appropriate tone
Clear passenger or staff instructions
Unsupported policy information
Protection of personal information
Conciseness and readability
Requirement for human approval

The peer review confirmed that the communication was clear, concise and appropriate for human review.

Key Learnings
A structured prompt produces more specific and useful aviation communication.
Professional email prompts should include Role, Task, Context, Constraints, Output Format and Tone.
BLUF places the most important information first.
PAS means Problem, Agitation and Solution.
PAS should be used carefully in aviation communication.
Unsupported compensation, refunds, meals, hotels and policy benefits should not be invented.
Audience determines the appropriate tone and level of detail.
AI-generated communication must be reviewed before professional use.
Official operational information should be verified before sending.
Human approval is required because AI-generated content can contain incorrect or unsupported information.
Conclusion

This lab demonstrated how Generative AI can assist in drafting professional aviation emails when it is guided by structured prompts and reviewed by humans.

Using the six-part prompt structure, BLUF, audience-based tone adaptation and the four-dimension review gate helps improve the accuracy, clarity, compliance and usefulness of aviation communication.

AI should support professional communication rather than independently approving or sending important business messages.
